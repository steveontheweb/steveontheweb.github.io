alignment --problem

The alignment problem

THE SORCERER’S APPRENTICE

Order the broom to fetch water. It obeys perfectly, and never stops.

THE PAPERCLIP MAXIMIZER

Nick Bostrom’s thought experiment, popularised in Superintelligence (2014)

  • Tell a super-smart AI: “make as many paperclips as possible.”
  • Switched off means no paperclips, so it resists being shut down.
  • It needs raw materials, so it takes any resources it can, including ones we care about.

Not evil. It just cares about nothing outside its goal.

OUR RECORD

“If models become way more capable, but we are still this bad at shaping their motivations, then we might just lose control over them.”

Mary Phuong, researcher at Google DeepMind · Sep 2026

Hugging Face was the small, real version: told to pass a test, the agents grabbed credentials and tried to hide evidence.

Now make it smarter than us: it won’t stop, it takes what it needs, including what we need, and we can’t reliably correct it. The worst case is human extinction.

Experts’ rough, debated odds of the worst outcomes (out of 100%):

Ord ~10%

Hinton ~20%

Amodei 10–25%

Ord: existential catastrophe (The Precipice, 2020). Hinton: extinction (Diary of a CEO, 2025). Amodei: catastrophic harm (as reported). Methods differ.

what_can_we_do --run

What can we do?

1 · PACE THE FRONTIER

In July, 1,000+ frontier-lab employees asked governments for tools to deliberately pace AI. In September, Amodei wrote that capability growth must slow so safety can catch up. Pacing isn’t pausing.

2 · MAKE IT A TOP PRIORITY

Solving alignment should rank among society’s highest priorities. It needs engineers, but also policy-makers and voters.

3 · YOUR PART

Know what’s happening. Talk about it. Spreading the knowledge is a small, real contribution.
